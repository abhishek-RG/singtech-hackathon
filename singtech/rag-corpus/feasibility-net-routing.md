# Site feasibility: On-Net, Near-Net, and Off-Net routing

Every proposal starts with fibre and 5G feasibility at each customer site. SingTech gives each site one status per technology.

## Statuses

- On-net: Singtel fibre or 5G coverage already reaches the building. Quote standard lead times.
- Near-net: coverage is close, but a short lateral build or indoor coverage work is needed. Add a site survey line and confirm build cost and lead time with the solution architect before quoting a go-live date.
- Off-net: no Singtel coverage. Service depends on a partner tail circuit or a new build. A partner tail is usually outside the Singtel SLA, so state the carve-out in the proposal or plan a managed alternative.

## Routing rules

1. All sites on-net: route as a standard Singtel Enterprise deal.
2. Any near-net site: add a site survey and a lead-time caveat. Do not commit a go-live date until the survey is back.
3. Any off-net site inside a CII or government scope: route through NCS with the network scope and make the SLA carve-out explicit.
4. Overseas sites (Johor, Bengaluru, Jakarta, Chennai, Mumbai): treat as off-net unless a confirmed partner path exists. For Johor, the TM Nxera campus is a candidate landing point (source: Nxera catalogue entry, https://www.itnews.asia/news/singtels-nxera-opens-58mw-data-centre-in-singapore-623491).
5. Sites with recurring strain (link saturation, slow syncs, Wi-Fi congestion) are upgrade candidates. Quote the measured strain in the proposal.
6. A site can be near-net for fibre and on-net for 5G. That combination is the strongest case for private 5G with edge processing.

## Lessons from sample accounts

- Off-net partner tails caused the Johor, Chennai, and Bengaluru incidents in the sample data, and no service credit applied. Customers notice; address it before renewal.
- Seats per site size the Mobility and security scope; strain notes size the bandwidth and 5G scope.
