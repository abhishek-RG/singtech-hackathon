# MNC consultative selling

Use this playbook for enterprise and MNC accounts with sites in more than one country: Singapore-headquartered groups with plants in Johor or Bengaluru, Indian companies expanding to Singapore or Southeast Asia, and MNCs present in both India and Singapore.

## Principles

- Lead with the customer's business problem, not a product. Ask what the operation must achieve in the next 12 to 24 months, then map each gap to an outcome.
- Sell bundles across the catalogue, not single products. The catalogue has 6 core products (5G+, CUBΣ, Cyber Security, IoT, Mobility, Quantum-Safe Network) and 3 extras (RE:AI, Nxera, NCS). Nothing else is sold.
- Match the buyer type. Risk-averse buyers get certifications, track record, and risk assessments. Innovation-driven buyers get a pilot. Efficiency-driven buyers get a business case with measurable outcomes. Partnership-driven buyers get a multi-year account plan.
- Map every stakeholder by role: the economic buyer (often the Group CFO or COO), technical buyers (Group CIO, CISO), users (Head of Operations), and the champion.

## Discovery questions

1. Which sites are opening, closing, or growing in the next 18 months, and in which countries?
2. Where does traffic break out to the internet today, and who owns the security policy for each site?
3. Which workloads carry regulated data (PDPA in Singapore, the DPDP Act in India, the Malaysian PDPA in Johor), and where must that data stay?
4. Which incidents in the last 12 months cost the business money or reputation?
5. How is a multi-country contract approved, and which legal entity signs and pays?

## Account plan structure

1. Now: what the account owns, its contract end date, open incidents, and billing status.
2. Next 6 to 12 months: likely needs from signals, site plans, and pain points.
3. Bundle path: the outcome bundle that closes each gap, with indicative ARR derived from catalogue ARR, never Singtel list pricing.

## Rules

- Every claim about a company carries a source link. With no source, say "No signal found".
- SingTech drafts; the account executive approves before anything reaches the customer or is saved as final in Salesforce. Emails are never sent automatically.
- Do not compete on price cuts. Defend value with SLA, coverage, and one accountable partner.
